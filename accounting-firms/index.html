The question

Was the market fragmented — or actually investable?

The initial thesis was attractive: thousands of firms, many small owner-led businesses and a market that looked ripe for consolidation.

But fragmentation alone does not create a good buy-and-build. The real question was whether there were enough suitable targets, whether integration economics worked and whether multiple uplift justified the complexity.

The goal was not to prove the thesis. It was to test whether the thesis deserved capital.
What I built

A proprietary market map and screening engine.

I created a structured dataset covering approximately 4,500 Dutch accounting firms and translated it into a repeatable investment-screening workflow.

  • Automated market data collection and enrichment
  • Multi-criteria target scoring
  • Geographic and market-fragmentation analysis
  • Acquisition candidate ranking
The model

From company data to platform economics.

The system modelled what a consolidating platform could look like over time, including acquisition pace, financing, integration, synergies and valuation uplift.

  • Organic growth plus bolt-on acquisitions
  • Purchase multiples and exit sensitivities
  • Cost synergies and revenue cross-sell
  • IRR, cash-on-cash and payback analysis
What the data showed

Highly fragmented. Not enough quality at scale.

The market contained many firms, but the strongest candidates were too limited in number and scale. The economics weakened once realistic integration effort, payback periods and target quality were included.

The decision

Sometimes the best outcome is to walk away.

No-go The investment thesis did not justify further commitment of time and capital.
